# Tutorial: How to Present Your SOL Project

This guide will help you prepare and deliver a professional presentation based on your SOL.tex manuscript and the GEESP-Angola project.

---

## 1. Define Your Audience and Goal
- Who are you presenting to? (e.g., academics, policymakers, funders, community leaders)
- What is your main objective? (e.g., inform, persuade, secure funding, share results)

## 2. Structure Your Presentation
A typical structure (10–15 slides):

1. **Title Slide**
   - Project title, your name(s), affiliation, date
2. **Problem Statement**
   - Why is rural electrification in Angola important?
   - Key statistics (e.g., electrification rates, SDG7 context)
3. **Project Objective**
   - What is GEESP-Angola? What gap does it fill?
4. **Methodology Overview**
   - Briefly explain MCDA-GIS, AHP, and LCOE
   - Visual: Flowchart of your framework
5. **Data and Criteria**
   - What data layers and criteria were used?
   - Visual: Table or icons for each criterion
6. **Site Selection Process**
   - How were sites prioritized? (weighted overlay, clustering)
   - Visual: Map of study area with priority zones
7. **Results**
   - Key findings (e.g., 3 priority zones, LCOE estimates)
   - Visual: Charts, maps, or summary tables
8. **Validation and Impact**
   - How was the model validated? What are the projected impacts (health, education, economy)?
   - Visual: Before/after or impact infographics
9. **Risk Mitigation and Sustainability**
   - How does your approach address common project risks?
   - Visual: Risk matrix or checklist
10. **Policy and Replicability**
    - How can this be scaled or replicated elsewhere?
11. **Conclusion and Recommendations**
    - Main takeaways and next steps
12. **Q&A**
    - Invite questions

## 3. Visuals and Tools
- Use clear, high-contrast slides (PowerPoint, Google Slides, or LaTeX Beamer)
- Include maps, charts, and icons for clarity
- Keep text minimal—use bullet points and visuals
- Practice with your visuals to ensure smooth transitions

## 4. Tips for Effective Delivery
- Practice your timing (aim for 10–15 minutes)
- Start with a strong hook (statistic, story, or question)
- Speak clearly and at a measured pace
- Engage your audience: ask questions, invite discussion
- Be ready to explain technical terms simply
- Prepare for common questions (e.g., data sources, limitations, scalability)

## 5. Optional: Supplementary Materials
- Prepare a 1-page handout or executive summary
- Have backup slides for technical details or extra data

---

**Good luck! Your work is impactful—present it with confidence.**
