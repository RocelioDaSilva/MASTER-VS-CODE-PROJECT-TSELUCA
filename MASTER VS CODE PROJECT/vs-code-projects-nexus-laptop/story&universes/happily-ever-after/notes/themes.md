# Themes & Motifs

## The American Dream
- The house, the cars, the stable family—these are the external markers of success.
- The story asks: what internal rot is hidden by that perfect exterior?

## Sacrifice
- The couple sacrificed careers, friendships, and ultimately their morality.
- Sacrifice is presented as both noble and destructive.

## Secrets
- The secret of the child’s parentage.
- The secret of the murder.
- Secrets as the glue that holds the family together—and the poison that will eventually tear it apart.

## Justice vs. Revenge
- The couple bypassed the legal system because they saw it as corrupt/unable to deliver justice.
- Their revenge, however, created new injustices (the frame, the murder, the blackmail).

## Corruption
- Police, prison system, and legal system are shown as exploitable for those with money.
- The blackmailer represents the ultimate corruption—justice turned into leverage.

## Memory and Trauma
- The wife’s repression of the assault.
- The husband’s realization that the child isn’t his.
- How both choose to bury the past rather than confront it.

## The Unreliable Happy Ending
- The title *Happily Ever After* is ironic. The story challenges the notion of a permanent happy state.
